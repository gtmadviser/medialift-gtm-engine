# Copy-paste prompts for ChatGPT or Claude in the browser

No coding agent? Use these in order. Start a new chat, paste **Prompt 0** once, then each prompt in turn. Keep the answers, they build on each other.

---

## Prompt 0: context (paste once)

```
You are my GTM advisor. Be concrete, short, and challenge me where I am vague.
Rules for any message you write: 50 to 70 words, open with the reader's situation,
no invented numbers, no em dashes, one soft ask.

Our startup: [name]
Product: [what it is, one sentence]
Who it helps: [who]
Problem it solves: [problem]
Who pays: [B2B / B2C / via partner]
Where we are: [customers, pilots, pre-orders, conversations]
Where we are stuck: [e.g. conversations but no deals]
```

## Prompt 1: market map

```
Help me count our market. What is the buying unit (company, department, household)?
Estimate how many exist in Germany / DACH, show sources and math.
Then tell me which motion fits that number: account-based (under ~300),
segment tests (300 to 5,000), volume (more), or communities/partners (B2C).
If the market is small, list the first 20 accounts by name with website and why they fit.
```

## Prompt 2: ICP card

```
Build our ICP card. Ask me for 3 best-fit and 3 worst-fit customers or conversations first.
Then write: who exactly (role + organization type), their pain in their words,
buyer vs user vs blocker, the trigger that says "now" and how to detect it,
the one channel to reach them, and disqualifiers. Max 6 sentences.
```

## Prompt 3: positioning

```
Write our positioning against the real alternative.
1) What would our customer do if we did not exist? List 3 real alternatives.
2) What do we have that those don't? 3) What value does that create, in the customer's words?
4) Who cares most about that value? 5) Which category makes it obvious?
Then one sentence: "For [who] who [pain], [product] is a [category] that [value].
Unlike [alternative], we [unique attribute]." Give me 3 versions and say which is strongest.
```

## Prompt 4: the first yes and pricing

```
Our conversations don't turn into deals. Here are the last 3 that stalled: [describe].
Diagnose the pattern. Then design a small, paid first yes:
B2B: a 4 to 6 week pilot with one success metric, scope, end date, price, decision rule.
B2C: a pre-order or deposit offer.
Add three price options (anchor high) and the exact question I ask at the end of a call.
```

## Prompt 5: partners (B2B2C / B2C)

```
Find 15 to 20 organizations that already own the relationship with our end customer
(publishers, retailers, associations, care providers, communities, platforms).
For each: why they would care, reach, ease of a yes, score 1 to 5.
Then write a 60-word partner pitch with a small pilot ask for the top one.
```

## Prompt 6: messages + A/B test

```
Write two outreach variants that differ in exactly ONE thing (opener, angle, or ask).
Each: LinkedIn connection note (max 300 characters, no pitch), DM after accept,
and a 2-step email. The ask is the first yes from Prompt 4.
State the hypothesis, the metric (meetings or pilots per 100 contacts), and the sample size.
```

## Prompt 7: research one account

```
Research [company] as a potential customer for us. Give me: what they do, size,
recent news and signals, the trigger for "now", the people to reach (buyer, user, blocker),
how our product maps to their pain, a fit score 1 to 5 with reasons,
and a first message following our rules.
```

## Prompt 8: next experiment

```
Turn everything above into ONE experiment until 26 November:
hypothesis, audience, channel, volume, metric close to money, owner, end date,
decision rule, and my first three actions tomorrow morning.
```
