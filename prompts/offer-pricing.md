# Offer and pricing: design the first yes

Goal: turn conversations into deals. Most stalled conversations have a next step that is too big, too vague, or free without a metric.

## 1. Diagnose the stall
Ask the founder for their last 3 to 5 conversations that did not close. For each: who was in the room (user or buyer?), what was the proposed next step, and where it stopped. Name the pattern.

## 2. Design the small, paid first yes
- **B2B:** a 4 to 6 week pilot with
  - one success metric the buyer cares about (hours saved, errors avoided, reach, revenue)
  - a fixed scope and an end date
  - a price (even a small one) or a clear conversion rule ("if metric X is met, we move to the annual plan at Y")
  - the decision maker and the decision date named upfront
- **B2C:** a pre-order, deposit, waitlist with payment, or a paid first module. Free signups don't count as a yes.
- **B2B2C / partners:** a co-marketing or revenue-share pilot with one partner, one channel, one metric.

## 3. Price test
Propose three options (e.g. small / recommended / premium) and anchor high. In the next 5 to 10 conversations, say the price out loud and log reactions: who flinches, who asks "is that per month?", who says yes. Price is a hypothesis.

## Output: `output/offer.md`
- The stall pattern in one sentence
- The first yes: scope, metric, duration, price, decision rule
- Three price options and what you'll say in the conversation
- The question you ask at the end of every conversation to get to the next step
